At 20 years old, barely even an adult, I thought I might die. This thought popped into my head suddenly. The natural instinct to fear for my life as my feet dangled 12,000 feet in the air burst through the giddiness of being back in the air.

I am cramped into a plane large enough to fit only 5 people, including the pilot, who was sane enough not to jump out of a perfectly good airplane. Bound to my back is Brad, the skydiving instructor, who would be responsible for pulling the parachute and keeping me alive for another day, and another jump. I take it as a good omen that my instructor shared the same name as my first car. I probably shouldn’t — the piece of shit Honda that it was.

I crane my neck, struggling to look back at Mom, who is strapped to a man twice her size. I want to reach out and give her a reassuring squeeze and let her know that everything would be okay. This is Mom’s first jump, and as much as she put on a brave face, I know she is scared. Every movement is restricted. My arms, legs, and chest are tightly bound by too many straps and clamps to count.

The door opens. The sound of rushing wind drowns out all sounds, all thoughts. I feel Brad shift behind me; I feel his thighs clench mine. He hooks our harnesses together, and it all tightens even more. I scoot to the edge of the door. I’m the first to jump. Feet dangle off the side as Brad does one last check. When all is clear, he begins his countdown.
