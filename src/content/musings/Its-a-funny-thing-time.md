As I sit here in this theater made of stones and memories, I can’t help but think about how time is always changing things. The tide can’t help but push waves up and down the shore, washing away the imprints of our feet we laid upon the soft, wet sand. The leaves of great big oaks and poplar trees can’t help but turn from vibrant green to burnt yellow, too long they spent basking in the summer sun.

My world is drowning in the symphony of beeping forklifts, the revving of diesel engines, and the whining of excavators and their chafing hydraulics. Sudden and deafening metal clangs ring out like cymbals – a period to a sentence, an ending I wasn’t ready for.

They are tearing down Jackson Hall to make way for something new. A dormitory to hold a new wave of dreamers, a fresh batch of bright, blooming students ready for what they’ve been told are the best four years of their lives. They might be.

I was once a dreamer – I still think I am. But I swear I hear a loud tick, a shuddering tock, and my body shivers. This place I’ve made a home for myself, this place I’ve decorated with meals shared with friends and late-night drives and laughter and hours daydreaming of what’s next, is now kicking me out.

You’ve been notified. You’ve been evicted. It’s time for you to go.

Another wall of Jackson Hall comes down as I sit here watching two freshmen pose to take a photo of themselves in front of Forest Theater, one of my favorite rooms in this house. Will they print them out and tape them to their dorm walls? Will they love this spot as I do?

There is beauty in this moment. It cuts through this self-pity like how a harsh ray of sunlight makes its way through the leaves. In the haze of dust kicked up in the destruction, no, construction, I see bright gold leaves drifting off to new places. In their wake, they leave behind a space for new growth come next spring. That has to be beauty, right?

Three years ago, I was living a stone’s throw from this spot, Stacy residence hall. I spent a lot of time here then. Not so much now. I remember going to the theater at midnight with my roommate. We were both wearing Carolina hoodies, not yet worn in, and stretchy pajama bottoms that snagged on branches as we walked down the path. Our bellies were stuffed with Spaghetti-O’s we ate in Dixie bowls on our dorm room floor. I remember lying down on the stone steps, gravel and sand scraping my back and tangling into my hair. We listened to Don McLean’s “Vincent” as we stared up at that starry, starry night. I drowned in the endless expanse, at the infinite possibilities of what was out there, at the infinite possibilities of me.

I’m thinking about Halloween. When I smoked with a guy who lived in the dorm next door. I remember sitting at the picnic tables right outside the theater, cold, wet wood leaving imprints on my bare thighs. I was wearing a scratchy pink tutu and sparkly fairy wings which straps were rubbing my shoulders raw. I remember the beat-up sneakers I wore that night, and at every party I went to freshman year. By that point, they were permanently caked in dirt and soaked through with cheap beer. We sat underneath the tree cover, sharing a poorly rolled cigarette that slowly unraveled with each pass. We stayed at that table until the sun rose the next morning. We hardly realized time was passing, not until our stories quieted, and in the silence, we heard the birds chirping.
