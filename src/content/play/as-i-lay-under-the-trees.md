Then I absentmindedly drew a fern leaf, and it was the first idea I really liked. From there I looked for ways to bring in quilt and textile elements, and landed on layered squares and stitch lines to build a quilted background. I wanted the finished print to feel like home, so I drew from fabric, sewing, sunshine, and nature. I mixed a deep purple from a pink I found on the shelves, added green and yellow, and used a deep blue to echo the cyanotype paper I’d been experimenting with. All of my inks were transparent, so the layers show through and the colors shift depending on what’s underneath. Every drawn element was done in marker."

    caption: "Printing took a long time. The background alone took 12 layers. I registered each print by hand with mylar and a ruler, so there’s some misalignment, along with a few bleeds and splotches. But I improved a lot along the way, with more even pressure, faster work, and a cleaner workspace. I also learned to print without a fixed plan. Not knowing which color or element came next let me play, experiment, and settle into a flow, which is when I do my best work."

This was my first screenprinting project, and my first time using emulsion. Coating a screen, exposing it to light, and washing out the image made me feel like a scientist. This piece was inspired by feelings of contentment, belonging, and the peace I feel when laying under the trees, watching the light filter through.
