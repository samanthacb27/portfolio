This project was a way for me to communicate the feelings I was having towards growing up. I made this at the end of my junior year, a year of much change. I’ve traveled and lived out of the country for the first time, moved into a new house, and discovered what I want to do post-grad. At the same time, I’ve been confronted by the reality that I am now an adult, with harsh realities I must face. My parents are getting older, and their health is getting worse. My sister and I have not lived in the same home for the last 6 years. And I’ve been thinking about how I am no longer the little girl I once was. 

While looking through photo albums from my childhood, I realized that I have little memory of the moments that shaped my childhood. I had a good childhood, and I wanted to memorialize those happy days and those dreams I had as a child. The birds came to me randomly mid-way through the project. I was thinking about myself as a child. The things I wanted, the dreams I had. And consistently, I dreamed of flying. I always loved the idea of flight, from birds to planes to superheros. I’ve always yearned for independence, the ability to be free to go wherever I wanted and to see whatever my heart desired. This is how I came up with the imagery of the birds and the text “when I grow up, I want to be a bird”. I decided to make this text lowercase and handwritten for an imperfect personal touch. The text whispers, it doesn’t scream. It’s a quiet reflection on growing up. 

I also decided early on to print on sheer fabric. I wanted to see what the light would
look like passing through printed images. But then my substrate took on a deeper
note. I decided to print onto the curtains that currently hang in my college bedroom.
Curtains, when pulled back, reveal the outside world. They serve as a means to see
outward. These curtains instead serve as a means to see inward, into myself and my
subconscious. They also have a history connected to my current self, the room I live in
now, as I’ve moved from childhood to adulthood. Having two curtains to print on gave me the idea to reflect the two contrasting feelings I am navigating and the two diﬀerent perspectives I have. I wanted to show the perspective of my younger self, who yearns to be older, independent, and free. I wanted to show the color of childhood, the wonder, and the joy. On the other hand, I wanted to my fear of getting older. I read this quote from John Steinbeck’s East of Eden, “It is an aching kind of growing.” I’ve never related to anything more. It does ache to grow. I fear the loss that comes with the passage of time and the loss of memories that is inevitable. I printed the second curtain without any birds and in one single muted violet. It is a quiet sadness. 

Printing on curtains was a challenge. For one, I had to make sure I had no ink on my
hands at all times when working with the fabric. There were so many opportunities to
smudge ink all over the fabric. I also had a hard time positioning the fabric under the
screen. With so much surface area to maneuver, it was a challenge to gather the fabric,
to line it up without it shifting, and to handle it without the wet ink folding onto other
sections of the fabric. I originally planned to print more than just one halftone layer of each photo on both curtains, but the shifting fabric made it almost impossible to register well. This challenge turned into my biggest successes when it comes to this piece. I love that the "hopeful" curtain is made with blue images, which are bright and hopeful. If I did not do that, I would never have printed the other curtain at all, which works so well as a monochromatic composition. I also love how some photos are darker than others, while some fade into the curtain. Like my memories, some are clearer than others.
