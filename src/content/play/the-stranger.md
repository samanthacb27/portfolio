I made this piece while studying abroad in Florence, Italy, as a study of Felice Casorati’s The Stranger (1930), which is housed in the Pitti Palace. For the assignment, I wanted to choose a work with a connection to Florence, something I could visit in person.

I was drawn to the painting’s muted palette of soft blues, greens, and reds, which creates a quiet, melancholic atmosphere, and to the unsettling tension in the scene. The figures feel frozen in time and stoic, yet their faces and body language are strikingly expressive.

The study challenged me in two ways. With multiple figures and a warped, looking-down perspective, the proportions were difficult to get right. I also had to capture small shifts of light and shadow across many different faces and objects. For this piece, I first made a light pencil sketch. Then I used a grisaille, painting values in black and white, which I then followed up with color.
